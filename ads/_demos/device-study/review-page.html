Apple's rules for its iPhone frames

What we may and may not do with option B

From Apple's App Store Marketing Guidelines, the Marketing Artwork Licence and the licence inside the download. Option C is our own phone, so these don't apply to it, as long as we never present it as an iPhone.

Allowed

  • Show the phone straight on, exactly as Apple drew it, in its own colours.
  • In video, after one shot of the whole phone, pan and zoom in to the app.
  • Fades and dissolves between shots.
  • Words, badges and Zee beside the phone.
  • The app as it really runs, with Emma's made-up account and a full status bar.
  • Free, for marketing an app that's on the App Store, while we're in the Apple Developer Program.

Not allowed

  • Rotating, tilting, flipping, spinning or animating the phone itself.
  • Adding shadows, reflections or highlights to it.
  • Anything coming out of (or going into) the screen, so no Pop out from this phone.
  • Cropping or covering any part of it; type on top of it.
  • A 3D or drawn copy of an iPhone, cases, other brands' phones next to it.
  • Smaller than 200 px tall on screen; a blank screen, the Home Screen or other apps.
  • Sharing or selling the frame file itself (it stays in our private library).
Credit line wherever an ad has legal copy: iPhone is a trademark of Apple Inc., registered in the U.S. and other countries.
  1. Pop out and the tilted-phone shots can't use Apple's frame. They either move to our 3D phone (option C) or change so nothing crosses the phone's edge.
  2. Every other phone shot can switch to Apple's frame as is: hold the whole phone, then push in or pan; cut between phones with a fade.
  3. The drawn phone copies the iPhone's island, which Apple's rules ask drawn phones not to do. One more reason to retire it for new ads.

Recommendation

B for every phone shot, C only where a shot must turn

  1. Apple's iPhone 17 Pro Max frame (option B) as the default phone. It is the most real look possible, it's free, and it fits our footage to the pixel. Shots open on the whole phone, then push in or pan; fades between them.
  2. Re-record the hero moments on the iPhone 18 Pro Max simulator, then move to the 18 frame, since Apple asks for the newest phone. The last full capture used about $0.78 of server time.
  3. Our 3D phone (option C) for the few shots that need a turn, a tilt or pieces leaving the screen, such as Pop out. Built in Remotion so footage plays frame by frame; Blender only for a single hero still like a poster or the website.
  4. Keep the drawn phone for old ads only. New ads use the new frame.

Your call: B, C or both (B by default, C for turning shots); which colour (Silver is the default); and whether to re-record on the 18 Pro Max now.
